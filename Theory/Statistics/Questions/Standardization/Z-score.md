**Q.** A nationwide competitive exam has a normally distributed score with a mean of $\mu = 500$ and a standard deviation of $\sigma = 50$. A student scores $575$ on the exam.

1. Calculate the $z$-score for the student.
2. Interpret what this $z$-score means in the context of the exam.
3. Determine what percentage of test-takers scored lower than this student.

---

**Ans.**:

### 1. Calculation of $z$-Score

Using the population $z$-score formula:

$$z = \frac{x - \mu}{\sigma}$$

Given:

* Student's score ($x$): $575$
* Population mean ($\mu$): $500$
* Standard deviation ($\sigma$): $50$

$$z = \frac{575 - 500}{50} = \frac{75}{50} = 1.5$$

---

### 2. Interpretation

* The student's score has a $z$-score of **$+1.5$**.
* This indicates that the student scored **$1.5$ standard deviations above the population mean**. Because the value is positive, their performance is higher than the average test-taker.

---

### 3. Percentage of Students Scoring Lower (Percentile)

To find the proportion of students who scored lower than $x = 575$:

* Look up $z = 1.50$ in the standard normal distribution ($Z$) table:

$$P(Z < 1.50) \approx 0.9332$$

* Convert to a percentage:

$$0.9332 \times 100\% = 93.32\%$$

Approximately **$93.32\%$** of test-takers scored lower than this student (placing the student in the $\approx 93^{\text{rd}}$ percentile).
